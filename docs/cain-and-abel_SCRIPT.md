# "The First Offering" — why God accepted Abel's offering and not Cain's — script v1 (2026-11-02)
Format: ~68 s vertical short, expressive narrator, no captions. English + Hindi.

## Facts (sources, KJV wording)
- Cain ("gotten", Genesis 4:1) was a tiller of the ground; Abel was "a keeper of sheep". Abel's Hebrew name, Hevel, means breath or vapour (the same word as "vanity" in Ecclesiastes 1:2). (Genesis 4:2)
- "Cain brought of the fruit of the ground an offering unto the LORD. And Abel, he also brought of the firstlings of his flock and of the fat thereof." (Genesis 4:3-4)
- "The LORD had respect unto Abel and to his offering: But unto Cain and to his offering he had not respect. And Cain was very wroth, and his countenance fell." (Genesis 4:4-5)
- Grain offerings were later commanded in God's law, so crops were not the problem in themselves. (Leviticus 2)
- "By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain." (Hebrews 11:4) "Cain... slew his brother... because his own works were evil, and his brother's righteous." (1 John 3:12)
- God to Cain: "If thou doest well, shalt thou not be accepted? and if thou doest not well, sin lieth at the door. And unto thee shall be his desire, and thou shalt rule over him." The Hebrew word for "lieth" pictures an animal crouching. (Genesis 4:6-7)
- "Cain rose up against Abel his brother, and slew him." "Am I my brother's keeper?" "The voice of thy brother's blood crieth unto me from the ground." (Genesis 4:8-10)
- Jesus' blood "speaketh better things than that of Abel". (Hebrews 12:24)

## Script (EN, ~232 words)
The first time two brothers brought God a gift, one of them ended up killing the other.
Cain is a farmer. His little brother Abel keeps sheep.
One day they both bring God an offering.
Cain brings some of his crops.
Abel brings the firstborn of his flock, and the best parts.
God looks at Abel's gift, and accepts it.
He looks at Cain's, and doesn't.
Wait. What's wrong with vegetables?
Nothing. Later, God's own law asks for grain offerings.
You see, the difference wasn't the gift. It was the heart.
Abel gave his first and his best. Cain just brought some. The Bible says Abel offered by faith.
It's like two kids making a birthday card for Mom. One spends all afternoon on it. The other signs a receipt.
Cain is furious. But God doesn't give up on him.
He says, do what's right and you'll be accepted. But sin is crouching at your door, and you must rule over it.
Cain doesn't. He takes Abel out to the field, and kills him.
God asks where his brother is. Cain says: am I my brother's keeper?
Funny thing: Abel was the keeper. Of sheep.
Abel's blood cried out from the ground.
But the New Testament says Jesus' blood speaks a better word than Abel's.
Abel's cried for justice. Jesus' cries for mercy.
