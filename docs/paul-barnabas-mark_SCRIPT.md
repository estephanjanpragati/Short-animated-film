# "The Quitter Who Wrote a Gospel" — original script v1 (2026-11-02)
Format: ~62 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, one steady narrator, story mode, no captions.

## Facts (sources)
- Barnabas was born Joseph, a Levite from Cyprus; the apostles nicknamed him Barnabas, "son of encouragement". (Acts 4:36)
- Mark (John Mark) was Barnabas's cousin (Greek *anepsios*). (Colossians 4:10)
- First journey, around AD 46-48: Paul and Barnabas sail from Antioch, take John Mark as their helper, and preach across Cyprus. (Acts 13:2-5)
- At Perga in Pamphylia, John left them and went back to Jerusalem. (Acts 13:13)
- Paul and Barnabas go on inland; at Lystra Paul is stoned, dragged out of the city and left for dead. (Acts 14:19)
- Some time later Paul proposes a second trip. Barnabas wants to take Mark; Paul thinks it unwise to take the man who
  deserted them in Pamphylia. "The contention was so sharp" that they parted. The Greek word is *paroxysmos*, the root
  of English "paroxysm". (Acts 15:36-39)
- Barnabas takes Mark and sails to Cyprus; Paul takes Silas through Syria and Cilicia. (Acts 15:39-41)
- Paul's route takes him to Macedonia and Philippi, the first church Acts records in Europe. (Acts 16:9-15)
- Later, Mark is with Paul as a "fellow worker" (Colossians 4:10, Philemon 24). From prison Paul writes:
  "Get Mark and bring him with you, because he is helpful to me in my ministry." (2 Timothy 4:11)
- Early church tradition (Papias, quoted by Eusebius, *Church History* 3.39) says Mark wrote down Peter's preaching:
  the Gospel of Mark. Most scholars think it was the first Gospel written.

## Script (~247 words, ~67 s at 3.7 w/s)
Two of the greatest missionaries in history broke up their team over one guy who quit halfway through his first trip.
It's around 47 AD, and Paul and Barnabas are setting sail to take the Gospel overseas.
They bring along a helper, Barnabas's young cousin, John Mark.
They cross the whole island of Cyprus, preaching town to town.
Then they land in Perga, with the big mountain roads ahead, and Mark packs his bag and goes home.
Paul and Barnabas keep going without him, and in one town Paul gets stoned and left for dead.
A few years later, they plan trip number two.
Barnabas says, let's take Mark.
Paul says, the guy who deserted us? Absolutely not.
The Bible calls it a sharp disagreement. The Greek word is paroxysmos, where English gets "paroxysm."
Luckily, Barnabas's nickname literally meant "Son of Encouragement."
Unluckily, that's exactly why they were fighting.
You see, they were both right about different things.
Think of two coaches and a player who walked off mid-game.
One says, the team can't carry him. The other says, if nobody gives him a second chance, he never comes back.
So instead of one team, there are now two.
Barnabas takes Mark back to Cyprus, and Paul takes Silas the long way, all the way into Europe.
Years later, writing from prison, Paul asks for Mark by name, because he's useful to me.
And the guy who quit halfway? Tradition says he wrote the Gospel of Mark.
