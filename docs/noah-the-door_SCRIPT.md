# "Only One Handle" — Noah, the door, and Revelation 3:20 — script v1 (2026-11-02)
Format: ~68 s vertical short, expressive narrator (user asked for more feeling than reel 1), no captions. English + Hindi.

## Facts (sources, KJV wording)
- God told Noah to build an ark 300 x 50 x 30 cubits (about 137 x 23 x 14 m) with ONE door in its side. (Genesis 6:15-16)
- A football pitch is 100-110 m long (FIFA Laws of the Game), so the ark was longer than a football pitch.
- Noah was "a preacher of righteousness". (2 Peter 2:5) "The longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing." (1 Peter 3:20)
- The animals came to Noah: "There went in two and two unto Noah into the ark". (Genesis 6:20, 7:9)
- People "were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and knew not until the flood came". (Matthew 24:38-39)
- Noah was 600 years old when the flood came. (Genesis 7:6)
- "And the LORD shut him in." God, not Noah, closed the door. (Genesis 7:16)
- Jesus: "I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved". (John 10:9)
- "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock: if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him". (Revelation 3:20)
- William Holman Hunt's painting "The Light of the World" (1851-53, Keble College, Oxford; a later version is in St Paul's Cathedral) shows Jesus knocking at an overgrown door that has no handle on the outside. Hunt meant a door that can only be opened from within.

## Script (EN, ~228 words)
The biggest boat of the ancient world had exactly one door, and Noah wasn't the one who closed it.
Noah is building a boat longer than a football field, on dry land, nowhere near the sea.
For years his neighbours watch him hammer.
He keeps warning them. They keep laughing.
Then the animals start showing up. Two by two. On their own.
And the neighbours? Still eating, drinking, throwing weddings. Jesus said they had no idea, until the flood came.
Noah walks in with his family, and the Bible says: the Lord shut him in.
Not Noah. God closed the door.
Luckily, that door kept the flood out.
Unluckily, it kept everyone else out too.
You see, for years that door stood wide open. Peter says God was patiently waiting.
Think of a friend knocking on your door in the rain. He can knock all night, but the handle is on your side.
That's what Jesus says in Revelation: I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door, I will come in.
In Holman Hunt's famous painting of that verse, the door has no handle on the outside.
He left it off on purpose.
Noah's door was shut by God. This one, he's waiting for you to open.
