# Reel: Should women cover their head in church?
**Series:** Have You Ever Thought? / कभी सोचा है? · **Day 1**
**Length:** about 65 s · vertical 1440×2560 · English + Hindi, same picture

## Facts this script stands on
| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| A woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonours her head | 1 Corinthians 11:5 |
| Women prayed and prophesied out loud in the Corinth church | 1 Corinthians 11:5 (the verse assumes it) |
| Long hair is given to her as a covering | 1 Corinthians 11:15 |
| "Judge for yourselves" | 1 Corinthians 11:13 |
| Corinth was a Roman colony; Paul wrote around AD 53–55 | Acts 18; standard dating |
| A Roman married woman's head covering signalled modesty and that she was married; going bare-headed in public could look shameful | Widely held by historians (e.g. Bruce Winter, *Roman Wives, Roman Widows*). The script says "many historians", not "everyone". |
| Catholic church law required it until 1983 and is now optional; Orthodox, Mennonite and many Indian churches still cover | 1917 Code of Canon Law, c.1262; dropped from the 1983 Code |
| God looks at the heart | 1 Samuel 16:7 |
| Each one should be fully convinced in their own mind | Romans 14:5 |

The reel does not declare one side wrong. It shows why each side reads Paul the way it does, then lands on what both agree on.

## ENGLISH (about 165 words)
| # | Scene | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 HOOK | A church in India. Two women walk in, one with her dupatta over her head, one without. An old aunty turns and stares. | Should a woman cover her head in church? Some say always. Some say not anymore. And both are reading the same Bible. |
| 2 SERIES | Title card: **Have You Ever Thought? · Day 1** | Have You Ever Thought? Day one. |
| 3 | Paul at his desk by lamplight, writing a letter. | In First Corinthians eleven, Paul writes: a woman who prays or prophesies with her head uncovered dishonours her head. |
| 4 | A Roman street in Corinth. A married woman with a veil over her hair, the crowd nodding with respect. | But look at the city. Corinth was Roman. Many historians say a married woman's veil there meant: I'm respectable, I'm married. Bare hair in public could look shameful. |
| 5 | Paul again, underlining a line. | So Paul cared about honour: not shaming your husband, or God, while you worship. He even says long hair is given to her as a covering, and tells them: judge for yourselves. |
| 6 | Split screen: left side Corinth, right side a modern church. | So Christians read it two ways. Many say the veil was Corinth's custom, and what stays is the heart behind it: honour, humility, order. Others, like Catholics, Orthodox, Mennonites, and many churches right here in India, say the covering itself is still a beautiful sign. |
| 7 TURN | Back in the Indian church, a glow over both women's hearts. | But here's what both sides agree on. A cloth never made anyone holy. God looks at the heart. |
| 8 | The two women now sit together in the same pew and smile. The aunty smiles too. | So if your church covers, cover with joy. If it doesn't, don't judge the sister who does. Let each one be fully convinced in their own mind. |
| 9 CTA | Logo end card. | Do women cover their heads in your church? Tell me in the comments. |
